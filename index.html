    Know the playbook

    Seven moves every FMCG brand pulls at the shelf. Once you can name them, they stop working on you.

    The Decoy Effect

    A third option is placed purely to make one specific pack look irresistible. Spot it: a size whose price sits just slightly under the bigger pack while giving you clearly less product. Take Nescafé Classic — the 1₹ sachet is roughly ₹500/100g, the 50g jar is ₹420/100g, and the 100g jar is ₹380/100g. The tiny sachet exists to make the jar look like genius. Sometimes the large genuinely is best — sometimes it isn't. Per-unit math decides, vibes don't.

    Flat multiplier packs

    Lay's Classic Salted: 52g for ₹20, 95g for ₹40, 147g for ₹60. Every option works out to roughly ₹0.38 per gram — identical value regardless of pack size. The bigger bag feels like a bulk deal but delivers zero savings: you just spend more money and eat more chips. Run any family of packs through the Compare tab: if the per-gram line is flat, buy the size you need, not the biggest one.

    Shrinkflation

    The sneakiest raise you'll ever pay. Price stays ₹10, pack quietly drops from 100g to 90g. Nothing changes at the counter, but your cost per gram jumped 11%. Maggi, Surf Excel, Colgate, Amul butter — serial offenders. Use Shrink Check when a pack feels lighter than memory; we quantify exactly how hard the hike hit.

    The "% extra free" illusion

    Free extra is added on top of a bigger base, so the saving shrinks: true % off = extra ÷ (100 + extra) × 100.

    • 25% extra free = 20% off
    • 50% extra free = 33.3% off
    • 100% extra free = 50% off

    Surf Excel "25% extra free" on the big pack sounds great — but that's only 20% off. Compare it against a straight "20% off" sticker on the smaller pack. The louder offer lost.

    Combo & bundle traps

    "Combo save ₹50!" — against what baseline? Add the shelf prices of the individual items yourself. Take a Maggi + Surf Excel combo on Amazon: listed as ₹199, individual prices total ₹210. That's only 5% off. Under ~5% combined saving, the combo exists to move slow stock, not to reward you. A good combo saves 15%+. Bundle Check does this arithmetic before the combo sign does it to you.

    Charm pricing (the ₹99 ending)

    ₹199 reads as "one-something" in your head while being effectively ₹200. That's why almost every Indian price ends in 99 or 9 — Amazon Great Indian Festival, Flipkart Big Billion Days, DMart shelves. Left-digit anchoring is the oldest trick in retail. Defense: round to the nearest hundred when budgeting, and always compare per-unit — decimals don't do psychology.

    Fake MRP anchoring

    The struck-through ₹299 may be a price nobody ever charged — invented to make ₹199 feel like charity. Verify with Claim Check using the printed MRP, not the sticker's "was" price. And remember: an MRP is a ceiling, not a suggestion. Many staples sell below MRP every day at DMart-style stores.

    The 20-second shelf checklist

    1. Convert everything to price per unit — mentally or here.
    2. Ignore the middle option until the ends are judged.
    3. Flat per-unit across sizes? Buy the small one.
    4. Translate "extra free" into true % off before comparing with % off stickers.
    5. Weigh the pack in your hand against last season if suspicious.
    6. Sum combo parts separately, always.

    FAQ

    What is a price per unit and why does it matter?

    It's price divided by quantity — per gram, ml or piece. Lay's 52g for ₹20 is ₹0.38/g, and the 147g for ₹60 is also ₹0.38/g. Same unit price, bigger spend. LootLens normalizes every pack and ranks them instantly so you never fall for the size trick.

    How do I know if a discount is actually real?

    Actual % = (original − sale) ÷ original × 100. If an Amazon sale says 40% off but the math says 25%, the claim is inflated. Claim Check runs this instantly, including the misleading "extra free" style offers on Surf Excel and similar products.

    What is shrinkflation?

    Same price, smaller pack — Maggi went from 70g to 60g at ₹14 and you silently pay 17% more per gram. Shrink Check quantifies the hidden hike from old vs new pack details.
